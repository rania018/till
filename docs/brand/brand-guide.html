Brand guide · Till

Till

What’s left till payday.

Till is a personal budget that counts the way you actually live: in pay cycles, in cash you can hold, and in hours of work. The brand is calm and exact. One plum, one typeface, numbers that are easy to read, and color only where it means something.

Calm  Exact  On your side

01 · The idea

A budget that speaks your units

Most finance apps talk in months and bank balances. Till talks in the three units a salaried person already thinks in. Every brand decision below follows from these three.

Left till payday

The one number that matters, shown first and shown big. Each “month” in Till is one pay cycle, from payday to the day before the next one.

Cash you can count

Two wallets, cash and card. A cash count catches what you forgot to log. The name nods to the till, the drawer where the cash lives.

Prices in hours of work

“Is it worth it?” turns 6,500 DA into 9 h 20 min of your time. This is Till’s signature feature, and it gets the one dark card on the home screen.

02 · The name

Why “Till”

Four letters, one syllable, two meanings that are both true. Till as in until payday, the countdown the home screen is built around. And till as in the cash drawer, the place you keep and count your money. It is easy to say in English, French and Arabic, works as a verb in copy (“Till it”), and the lowercase word is made only of stems, which lets the wordmark be drawn rather than typed.

Till recommended

Tagline: “What’s left till payday.” Short descriptor for stores: “Budget by pay cycle, cash and hours of work.”

Pace

Alternative. Centers on the daily safe-to-spend figure and the budget-pace line in Stats. Clean, but it says less about cash and hours.

03 · Logo

A “t” whose crossbar is a budget bar

The mark is a lowercase t drawn with one stroke weight. Its crossbar is a progress bar: the solid part is what you have spent, the faint part is what is left till payday. The same t opens the wordmark, where the dot of the i is a coin in Plum. Nothing else is decorated.

Rules

  • Clear spaceOne stroke width (14 units) on every side
  • Minimum sizeMark 20 px · Wordmark 64 px wide · Lockup 120 px
  • ColorsDamson, white, Plum or Lilac only; coin in Plum or Lilac
  • Bar fillFixed at 60% in all static uses; the live app may animate it
  • NeverGradients, shadows on the mark, outlines, rotation, stretching, typing “Till” in another font next to it
04 · Color

One plum, used sparingly

Plum is the brand, and it appears in small doses: the coin, the primary button, the progress bar, a link. It is rare in finance, warm without being loud, and deep enough to carry white text anywhere. Most of every screen is Paper, white and Damson, with neutrals that lean faintly violet so the accent feels at home. Status colors (good, careful, over) keep their meaning because nothing else is green, amber or red. All text pairs below meet WCAG AA; ratios are measured.

Status and chart colors are functional, not brand. They never appear in the logo, on the splash screen or in marketing, and the brand never borrows them, so green always means good and red always means over.

05 · Typography

Manrope, heavy for numbers, light on words

One family for everything, which keeps the app small and fully offline. Manrope’s geometric forms match the drawn wordmark, and its tabular figures line up in lists. Amounts are the hero: set them ExtraBold with tight tracking, and keep labels small and quiet.

Use font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums wherever digits sit in columns. Amounts always carry the currency once per screen, not on every line.

Arabic and French

Manrope has no Arabic glyphs. For an Arabic interface, pair it with IBM Plex Sans Arabic at the same weights; it shares Manrope’s low contrast and open counters. French works in Manrope as is.

06 · Voice

Calm, plain, on your side

Till talks like a friend who is good with money: states the fact, says what it means, never scolds. No exclamation marks, no finance jargon, no guilt. Second person, present tense, short sentences.
